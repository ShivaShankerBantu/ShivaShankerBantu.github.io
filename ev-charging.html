EVCS

An EV charging app for India that sends drivers only to chargers that actually work, shows the full price before they plug in, and lets them pay per session by UPI.

Role
Sole designer
Platform
Mobile app, iOS and Android
Users
EV drivers in India
Timeline
15 weeks in 2019, revisited in 2026

A personal project. I designed the first version in 2019. In 2026 I rebuilt it from scratch around what EV drivers in India struggle with today: new research, a new identity and every screen redesigned.

Try the prototype Case study (PDF) The prototype is clickable from sign-up to receipt. The PDF adds the user journey and user flow.

The problem

In 2019 the problem was simple: there weren't enough chargers. By 2026 India has tens of thousands of public chargers, but drivers still can't trust them. A charger the app shows as available is often broken, every network wants its own app and wallet, and the price only becomes clear after you plug in.

So the question changed from “where is a charger?” to “which charger will actually work, and what will it cost me?”

Who it's for

Three personas built from the 2019 interviews and the 2026 research.

Persona 1 / Cab driver
Ravi

Drives 200 km a day for a ride-hailing app in Hyderabad. Every hour lost at a broken charger is an hour without fares.

Pain points
  • Drives to chargers the app says are free, and finds them broken
  • Waits in queues without knowing for how long
  • Has money stuck in several wallets
Persona 2 / IT professional
Priya

Lives in an apartment where she can't install a charger, so she depends on public ones every week.

Pain points
  • Only sees the real price after charging
  • Sessions that fail halfway, with slow refunds
  • Isn't sure which connector fits her car
Persona 3 / Retired, road trips
Mr. Rao

Drives to see family in other cities. Comfortable with UPI, but not with an account and a wallet in every app.

Pain points
  • Worries about finding a working charger on the highway
  • Finds wallet top-ups and refunds confusing
  • Wants to plan his stops before he leaves

How the design answers it

Verified working, not just “available”
Each charger shows when it last completed a charge and how often sessions succeed. Drivers confirm it after every session, which keeps the badge honest.
One app across networks
Chargers from every network on one map, and you can browse without creating an account.
No wallets
Pay per session by UPI. You approve a ₹450 block, only the energy you use is charged, and the rest is released automatically.
The full price, upfront
Price per kWh including GST, plus the cost and time for your car, before you plug in. Idle fees are explained before they start.
Your car sets the filters
Add your car once, and you only see connectors that fit it.
A plan for when it fails
If a session fails, nothing is charged, the block is released, and the bay is flagged for other drivers.

Visual direction

Before any screens, a mood board set the feel: calm at night, one electric accent, and status you can read at a glance. Everything on it was made for this project.

A new identity

The 2019 logo was redrawn on a proper grid. The map pin and lightning bolt stay, with dark ink and a bright volt green, so the green, amber and grey status colours stand out on every screen.

AFind

A charger you can trust

Pins show the price and live status at a glance, and the filters start with your car's connector. The station page answers the questions drivers ask before they drive there: does it work right now, what will it cost my car, and which bay fits?

BCharge

Reserve, charge and pay, without a wallet

A free reservation with a grace period, a QR scan to start, a UPI block instead of a top-up, and a receipt that splits out the GST.

CWhen it goes wrong, and road trips

Designed for the bad day too

When a charger drops a session, the driver sees straight away that nothing was charged and where their money is, and gets a working bay one tap away. Ask EVCS, an AI trip planner, plans stops along a route using only chargers that are verified working.

DFirst run

Three steps, or none

Drivers already have too many charging apps, so signing up is short: a phone number, a code read from SMS, and your car. Or skip it all and browse the map.

Outcome

This is a concept, not a shipped product. In 2019, testing with 5 users found the screens easy to understand and the reservation option useful, and asked for prices on every station. The 2026 redesign closes that gap and answers the problems drivers report today.

Next, I'd test the prototype with EV drivers in Hyderabad, starting with cab drivers, and measure how quickly they find a working charger and whether they trust the “Verified working” badge.
